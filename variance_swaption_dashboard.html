Analysis

Why are the two prices so different?

The Bergomi eq. 7.41 approximation σV = 2ν̂ is a first-order linearisation valid only for small ν. At ν = 150% it breaks down completely. The forward VS variance is an arithmetic average of lognormal forward variances ξuT1 over u ∈ [T1, T2] — a basket of lognormals. Jensen's inequality and the non-lognormality of the basket push the true price far above the linearised estimate. The MC implied vol of 74.07% captures this convexity; the BS 5.97% does not.

Martingale check

Under the risk-neutral measure, forward variances are martingales: E[ξuT1] = ξu0 = 0.04 for all u. The simulation gives E[V̄] = 0.039941, within one standard error of 0.04, confirming the Cholesky decomposition, Ito integral accumulation, and trapezoidal integration are all correct.

Interpretation

The ATM variance swaption costs 0.8222% of notional (in variance units) under the full Monte Carlo simulation. The Black-Scholes approximation underestimates by a factor of 12. The reliable price is the Monte Carlo result. The BS result is useful only to illustrate the breakdown of the small-ν approximation at the extreme vol-of-vol calibrated in Set I.
